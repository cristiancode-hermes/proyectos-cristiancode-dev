Sector 03 · Field science

Science the surface can't see.

Every expedition ships a research question. A few of the ones we keep returning to.

Hydrothermal vents

Chemosynthesis

In 1977, nobody believed an ecosystem could live without sunlight. The vent fields we survey still rewrite the textbooks — tube worms, yeti crabs, microbes that eat rock.

400 °C vent fluids 2 m tube worm colonies
Bioluminescence

Light in the dark

Three quarters of the animals in the deep make their own light. We map the patterns, the colours and the timing — and we have only just begun to catalogue them.

92% of deep species glow 3,412 species catalogued
Carbon pump

The ocean's memory

The deep is the planet's largest carbon store. What sinks, stays. Our sediment cores trace 800,000 years of climate — and tell us what we are doing to it now.

38 Gt CO₂ absorbed yearly 800 kyr of archived climate

Sector 04 · The log

Expedition log, 2024–2026.

Raw entries from the ships — dates, depths and what came back up with us.

14 May 2026−10,928 m

Trench-6, Mariana

Full-ocean-depth lander returned with the first images of a hadal snailfish swarm at 10,700 metres. Forty-one new observations logged before the batteries died.

02 Feb 2026−4,050 m

Guaymas Basin

Baited cameras recorded a forty-minute aggregation of yeti crabs at a new vent field. Samples under analysis in Bergen.

17 Sep 2025−6,000 m

Kermadec Trench

AUV Lamprey completed a 210-kilometre grid — the first continuous map of a hadal fault escarpment, made while the surface crew slept.

03 Apr 2025−1,200 m

Porcupine Seabight

Cold-water coral garden, four hectares, previously unmapped. Proposed to the regional marine park authority for protection.

We go down because the answers are down there. Every time we come back, the map of the planet has changed a little — and that little is enormous.
Dr. Ingrid Sørensen Chief scientist, Pelagia Expeditions

Sector 07 · Small print

Asked on deck.

None. Every berth includes a two-week training package before you board, and on the ship you are crew — you learn the watches, the sampling and the cameras from the people who do this professionally. The only requirement is being comfortable at sea.

Our submersible is certified to 11,000 m and has made ninety-plus dives past 9,000 m without a critical incident. Every dive follows a written plan, a dedicated surface team and a 72-hour pre-dive checklist. The ocean is not safe; we make the operation as safe as engineering allows.

It is published openly within twelve months — raw sensor logs, video, sample metadata — under a CC-BY licence. You get early access as crew, and your name goes in the dataset credits.

Yes. Up to 90 days before departure you are refunded in full; up to 30 days, 50%. If the ship can't sail — weather, politics, a pandemic — you are refunded entirely and offered priority for the next season.

Shelf berths start at €4,200, Abyssal at €9,800 and Hadal at €18,500 — all-inclusive: ship, training, meals, dive time and insurance. Fellowships cover the science berths, never the paid ones.
